## 5. How I'd run the co-prime account (the working model)

> This is how I'd operate the Prime / Core AE split on a named Malaysia account — and how the language gap gets absorbed structurally rather than personally.

**Principle: shared ownership, split lanes, one number.** The Core AE owns the broad Salesforce relationship and the overall account number; I own and carry quota for the **Tableau / analytics** line. We're co-primes — neither reports to the other. My job is to make the Core AE *want* me in their account, not to compete with them for it.

**Lane split on a named account:**
- **Core AE (likely locally-based, Bahasa-fluent):** relationship owner, platform-level access, the C-suite conversation, and the Bahasa conversational layer in Bahasa-first accounts.
- **Me (Prime Tableau AE):** the analytics specialist — discovery on data and BI pain, the Tableau demo/architecture conversation, the analytics business case, and the Tableau line of the forecast.
- **Shared:** account plan (co-authored), quarterly business reviews, partner and SI engagement, and the negotiated close.

**How the Bahasa gap is absorbed by the model, not by me faking it:**
- Bahasa-first conversations (intro, relationship maintenance, local stakeholder management) → **Core AE leads, I join and learn.**
- English-medium procurement (MNCs/GLCs like MISC, Petronas, banks — where buying actually happens in English) → **I lead.**
- I build my own Bahasa in parallel — functional within one quarter, conversational within two — but the model never *depends* on me being fluent, because the Core AE is there by design.

**First 90 days on a named account:**
1. **Days 1–30 — map and align:** co-build the account plan with the Core AE; map the data and BI buying centre (CDO, analytics leads, finance ops); agree the lane split and quota-credit rules up front so there's no friction later.
2. **Days 31–60 — qualify and prove:** run Tableau-specific discovery on the analytics use cases the Core AE's platform deal *isn't* covering; land a proof-of-value / pilot scoped to a single pain point.
3. **Days 61–90 — pipeline the analytics line:** convert the pilot into a multi-year Tableau commitment; forecast the analytics line separately in CRM so the co-prime contribution is visible to leadership.

**Why this works for Tableau specifically:** the analytics opportunity *is* the part of the account the Core AE can't sell — they don't carry Tableau. That makes me additive, not competitive, which is the single most important thing to get right in a co-prime seat.
